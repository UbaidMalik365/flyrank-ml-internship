Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring

A decision-support analysis for identifying content pages that may warrant refresh or review.

Abstract

This project asks whether observable search and content signals can be used to rank pages that may warrant content-refresh review. I built a baseline rule and then trained a Decision Tree using observable content and search-performance features from the FlyRank internship dataset. On the Week-5 test split, the Decision Tree achieved a measured Precision@50 of 0.62 compared with 0.46 for the Week-4 baseline. An additional grouped-by-client validation produced Precision@50 of 0.58, compared with 0.70 under the Week-5 random split, showing that validation design materially affects the measured result. The output is therefore best treated as directional decision-support for human review rather than as proof of causal ranking or traffic improvement.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams need practical ways to decide which pages deserve attention first. A simple rule can provide a useful starting point, but it may miss interactions between multiple signals.

The research question for this project is:

Can observable content and search-performance signals improve the ranking of pages for potential content-refresh review compared with a simple baseline rule?

The intended decision is prioritization: which pages should a human reviewer examine first.

2. Data

The analysis used the FlyRank ML Internship dataset. The working dataset contained 30,000 rows and 44 columns.

The available fields included content characteristics, search performance, engagement signals, content age, freshness information, click-through rate, average position, and trend information.

The analysis focused on observable features available for the modeling task. Client names, domains, private queries, and other identifying information were not included in the public-facing analysis.

3. Methodology

Baseline

The Week-4 baseline used a simple rule-based ranking approach. Its measured Precision@50 on the Week-5 comparison was 0.46.

Model

A Decision Tree was selected because it can represent non-linear relationships between observable signals while remaining relatively easy to inspect.

Features included observable content and search-performance signals such as content age, days since last update, impressions, average position, CTR, and word count.

Validation

The Week-5 model was initially evaluated using a random train/test split. This produced 24,000 training rows and 6,000 testing rows. The training and testing declining rates were both approximately 0.542.

A later audit used a grouped-by-client split. This produced 25 training clients and 7 testing clients, with zero shared clients between the two groups.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The model identifies pages that resemble observed declining patterns; it does not prove that refreshing a page will improve its performance.
  • Precision@50 measures ranking quality for the selected label, not business impact.
  • The difference between random and grouped validation shows that performance may not generalize equally across clients.
  • The analysis does not establish a causal relationship with search engine rankings.
  • Human review remains necessary before taking content actions.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The model output should be used as a review queue rather than an automatic publishing system.

Priority Action Reason
1 Review for refresh Multiple observable signals indicate that the page may warrant attention.
2 Inspect freshness Older content or longer time since update can support a refresh review.
3 Inspect search performance Review impressions, CTR and position together rather than relying on one metric.
4 Human review Confirm that the recommended action makes sense for the actual content before changing it.

What should NOT be automated

The system should not automatically rewrite, delete, merge, publish, or make other irreversible content decisions. Those actions require human review and additional context.
